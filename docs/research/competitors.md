# Research: competitors

## Summary

Competitive teardown as of 2026-09-28. META MUSE (launched 2026-09-08): the reference for trust architecture. Each user gets their own VM. A separate "Sentinel" is the only authority for egress and connector calls (allow, deny or ask). The agent never sees credentials: it holds surrogate tokens and the real secret is swapped in at the network boundary. Approval prompts arrive as a system dialog in the app, not as chat text, and can be scoped one-time, session, task, time-bounded or perpetual. Purchases use Stripe single-use cards. There is an audit log, "forget" for memories, and background tasks. Weak points: US-only (TechCrunch says US+Canada), 18+, and a payment card is required even on the free tier. It is 1:1 only. Documented failures: it recommended restaurants closed for years, invented phone numbers, and claimed it could haggle on Marketplace when it cannot. Amazon blocks it and Target blocks checkout. Users hit approval fatigue ("throughput capped by how often you look at your phone"). Privacy is the biggest problem: data-grabby suggestions, a reported sync of 187k iMessage rows plus a made-up explanation for it, and a Mac zero-day. Its Muse Spark 1.3 model trails Claude Opus 5 on agent benchmarks. MIRA (@mira, TOP): the distribution leader on Telegram, with ~1.17M MAU (June 2026), 175K groups, and over 1/3 of new users discovering it through groups. It is wide but shallow: Composio integrations, Skills, a daily digest, a creator studio, a TON wallet (testnet only), and a Private Mode running Qwen3-32B. ~95% of its tokens go to cheap open-weight models (MiniMax M3, gpt-oss-120b). Its own H1 report admits only 1 in 250 active users connects Gmail or Calendar, and reminders or automations are ~2% of use; it calls the bottleneck "onboarding, not demand". Its docs say group facts are visible to members, which contradicts its "private by design" claim. There is no documented approval UX, audit log or memory viewer. Pricing: Free, Pro from $27, Pro Max $99, plus token packs. FOLK (Nozomio Labs): the richest feature set among personal agents. Each user gets an isolated cloud computer. Its browser hands login, 2FA and payment steps off to the user's phone through a live link. It has a knowledge-graph memory with a "brain" viewer and delete, memory import from ChatGPT/Claude, and nudges with a daily budget plus /nudges controls. It also offers phone calls, live-location reminders, a meeting notetaker, money-back disputes via read-only Plaid, groups on Telegram and iMessage, "crew" agent-to-agent coordination, and /approve, /deny and /yolo. Complaints: the lowercase tone is unfit for sending email; users call memory "an undo problem"; it is young with thin reviews (Reddit 5.2/10 per Toolsplorer); its own pages give contradictory pricing ($9.99 vs $20 Pro, 20 msg/day vs unlimited); it is personal-life only; key features are paywalled; signup goes through a web dashboard. POKE (adjacent): made famous by an onboarding "magic" of knowing and roasting you from your inbox, plus haggling over its own price. It is unreliable on dates and time zones, ~30% of its nudges are noise, and it is slow. OTHERS: GPT4Telegrambot (~3M users, model aggregator, 600 Stars/mo, no actions). TeleClaw (group @mention bot, $1 per 1k credits; the "best bots" list recommending it is written by its own maker). OpenClaw (self-hosted, a security disaster: CVE-2026-25253, 21k exposed instances). TELEGRAM now gives Gora primitives that none of these three fully use: guest mode (10.0), Chat Automation (business bots without Premium), bot-to-bot, sendMessageDraft/sendRichMessageDraft streaming with can_stop, Rich Messages, ephemeral per-user messages in groups (10.2), topics in private chats, Managed Bots (personal named bots), and Stars subscriptions. What "better" means for Gora: Muse-grade trust architecture, delivered natively in Telegram, globally, with no card, frontier quality (Claude Opus 5), tools connected within the first minute, groups that don't leak, and proactivity that has a budget and verifies its facts.

- **[verified] Muse – security architecture (the bar to match)**: Per-user Muse Secure VM. The agent runs in a systemd-nspawn 'runtime cell' where root maps to an unprivileged host user. Sentinel is a separate host-side agent and the sole permission authority for connector actions and all network egress; 'Muse proposes actions, but only Sentinel can grant permission' (allow, deny or ask the user). The authd credential service mints surrogate tokens and real secrets are injected at the network boundary; privsep workers hold per-connector credential allowlists. The browser subagent receives an accessibility-tree snapshot rather than raw DOM and cannot run JS. The email connector strips OTPs, password-reset links and magic links using deterministic filters plus a classifier. Untrusted input is labeled, with ensemble injection classifiers and deterministic boundaries. Approvals go to the client UI as a system dialog 'not via their conversation with Muse' so injected text cannot forge consent. Grant scopes: one-time, session, task, time-bounded or perpetual. Known merchants require exact-detail approval every time; new merchants get Stripe single-use card numbers tied to merchant, amount and time. Confidential VM with user-held keys is coming later in 2026. Bug bounty up to $300k ($130k for single-user prompt injection). Meta admits 'Prompt injection remains an open problem'. _(source: research.meta.ai/blog/security-and-safety-for-ai-agents-our-approach-with-muse; charonhub.deeplearning.ai/how-to-secure-agents-for-the-masses)_
- **[verified] Muse – feature list**: Turns a goal into a plan, then advances it autonomously. Browser opens pages, fills forms, books and negotiates. Background tasks keep running after the app closes and 'come back when something changes or when it needs approval'. Audit trail of 'everything it has done and plans to do'. Per-app permissions (read-only vs send). Users can 'forget' specific memories. Proactive example: an Instagram-saved recipe reel becomes a grocery list. Stripe Link checkout; Shop Pay and 1Password coming. Connectors: Gmail, Google Calendar, Workspace, Outlook, OpenTable, Ticketmaster, Spotify, Apple Health, Peloton, Plaid, Function Health, GitHub, Granola, Notion, Tailscale, Facebook and Instagram, plus retail partners Best Buy, Gap, Sephora, Walmart, Wayfair, Expedia and Instacart. ## Implications for Gora

- GAP #1 (value 10, feasibility 9). Connect tools within the first minute, or deliver value without connecting. Mira: only 1 in 250 users ever connect Gmail or Calendar, and reminders are 2% of use. Muse requires a Meta account and a card. folk sends Telegram users out to a web dashboard. Gora: offer connection only at the moment it is needed ('I can check your calendar for that → [Connect Google]') via a Mini App OAuth that returns straight to the chat. Show a concrete payoff within 60s of connecting: one unanswered important email, or tomorrow's conflict, with a drafted fix on an approval card. Track 'connected a tool in session 1' as the north-star activation metric.
- GAP #2 (10/9). Approvals that can't be spoofed and don't cause fatigue. Muse's one weakness here is throughput capped by approval frequency, plus 'perpetual write at 11pm'. folk uses typed /approve and /yolo; Mira's approval flow is undocumented. Gora: render each action as an inline-keyboard approval card showing the exact diff (recipient, subject, body, amount, calendar slot). Bind approvals to a server-side pending_action id plus an HMAC in callback_data. Accept them only from a callback_query by the owner user_id; never from free text or model output (Telegram's version of Muse's out-of-band dialog). Offer Muse's grant scopes as buttons: Once / This task / 24h / Always for this recipient. After N identical approvals, suggest auto-approve (a trust ladder). Deliver non-urgent approvals as a batched digest.
- GAP #3 (9/9). A visible 'What I know / What I did' ledger. Muse has an audit log but opaque inferences and a made-up explanation of its Messages access. folk's 'brain' lives on a web dashboard. Mira has neither. Gora: a Mini App with a Ledger (every tool call, data read and action, with timestamps and sources) and a Memory tab (each fact with the message it came from, plus edit and forget). Add a /why command that explains the provenance of any answer or nudge. Store all of it in SQLite (the actions and memory_facts tables carry source_message_id).
- GAP #4 (9/8). Verified facts and honest capabilities. Muse recommended places closed for years, invented phone numbers, and claimed Marketplace haggling it couldn't do; Poke gets dates and time zones wrong. Gora: before recommending a place or business, verify it is open and check its hours with a search or fetch tool. Keep a capability registry, so the model says 'I can draft this; you send it' up front instead of overpromising. Resolve time zones deterministically from user settings and location and echo them in confirmations ('Tue 14 Oct, 15:00 Almaty time'). Use Claude Opus 5, which beats Muse Spark on agent benchmarks, for agentic turns.
- GAP #5 (9/6, the Telegram-unique moat). Hear the conversations where decisions happen. Muse only knows your connected apps. folk 'only sees the conversation you have with it'. Mira only offers Telegram Business delegation. Chat Automation (Bot API 10.0: business bots without Premium; the user picks which chats) lets Gora, with explicit per-chat consent, catch commitments ('I'll send it tomorrow' → follow-up), flag unanswered DMs, and draft replies in the user's voice for approval. Default to draft-only, never auto-send. Show a per-chat access list in the Mini App.
- GAP #6 (8/9). Groups that don't leak, plus private answers inside groups. Mira's docs contradict themselves (group facts are public), and folk just stays quiet. Gora: use guest mode (answerGuestQuery) for one-off help in any chat without joining. Use ephemeral messages (Bot API 10.2) for answers only the asker sees, e.g. 'what did I miss?' or 'how does this affect my calendar?'. Keep a visible, pinned 'group memory' the group can edit. Enforce strict private↔group↔group isolation in code (memory rows keyed by scope), and reply only when mentioned by default, since noise is the #1 complaint about group bots.
- GAP #7 (8/7). Multiplayer coordination that actually converges. folk's 'crew' and scheduling and Mira's decision tracking are the benchmarks. Gora: native polls (media, members_only, revoting, allow_adding_options), checklists with owners, a cost-split ledger and RSVP tracking. Gora-to-Gora negotiation between users via bot-to-bot or internal routing: propose times from each member's calendar, where each owner approves their own slot. Build the funnel Mira proved: over 1/3 of Mira's new users first meet it in a group, so every group action should carry a 'use me privately' DM deep link.
- GAP #8 (8/9). Proactivity with a budget and a reason. Poke's nudges are ~30% noise. Muse's 'suggestions' read as data grabs. Mira's proactive skills are popular but hard to find. Gora: a proactive engine with an explicit daily budget (user-tunable; default ≤3 unprompted messages/day). Each nudge carries 'why now' and one-tap Do it / Snooze / Never this kind, and learns from dismissals. Never ask for more data unless it unblocks a goal the user stated. Offer a morning brief preview during onboarding. Provide /quiet and a nudge-settings screen in the Mini App.
- GAP #9 (8/8). Background tasks with live progress, not a black box. Muse returns only when something changes. folk's watchers are paid and 1:1 only. Mira caps triggers at 10. Gora: durable jobs in SQLite (a jobs table plus a scheduler loop in the single Node process), each with one status message edited in place. Stream with sendMessageDraft or sendRichMessageDraft and can_stop, and handle Update.stopped_message_generation to cancel. Give each long task its own private topic (topics in private chats, 9.3/9.4). Include a generous free quota of watchers (price, page, inbox, flight).
- GAP #10 (7/9). Telegram-native rich output instead of walls of text. Muse's travel results were text-only and reviewers said native apps were better. Gora: Rich Messages (tables and details blocks for comparisons, map blocks, a Thinking block for progress), sendVenue and location pins, inline buttons (colored with the style field), and Mini App cards for hotels, flights and options, with 'open booking link' deep links.
- GAP #11 (8/6). Frontier quality at a consumer price. ~95% of Mira's tokens go to cheap open-weight models. Gora: run claude-opus-5 on agentic and tool turns. Cut cost with prompt caching (a stable system prompt, tool definitions and memory summary as the cached prefix), compaction of old turns, and cheap routes (small or fast settings) for classification, summaries and group digests. Meter usage transparently in the Mini App.
- GAP #12 (7/9). No card, global, pay in Telegram. Muse is US-only and needs a card even for Free; folk and Poke take cards for trials; Mira sells opaque token packs. Gora: a real free tier with no card, subscriptions in Telegram Stars (handle the BotSubscriptionUpdated update), regional pricing, and a plain-language 'what costs what'. Never paywall the trust features (ledger, forget, approvals).
- GAP #13 (7/4, phase 2). Doing things on sites without APIs. folk's live-browser hand-off and Muse's vaulted credentials are the benchmarks, and both still hit Amazon/Target blocks and auth loops. v1 Gora: never hold passwords. Produce prefilled deep links and booking URLs plus drafted forms the user finishes in one tap, and be honest when a site blocks agents. Phase 2: a sandboxed remote browser with a Mini App live hand-off for login, 2FA and payment, with credentials injected outside the model context (a Muse-style surrogate token pattern).
- GAP #14 (6/9). Memory portability and 'undo'. folk's import from ChatGPT/Claude is a strong instant-personalization trick, and its users describe memory as an undo problem. Gora: paste-to-import during onboarding, showing the extracted facts with ✓/✗ before saving. Per-fact forget. An incognito toggle that writes no memory. Full JSON export. 'forget everything from this chat'. Make memory granularity a selling point.
- GAP #15 (6/9). Security posture as a product feature. OpenClaw's CVE-2026-25253 and exposed instances, and Muse's Mac zero-day, made users wary. Gora: build a code-level 'Sentinel' policy layer between the model and tools. It keeps an allowlist of side-effecting tools and enforces approval in code, not in the prompt. It also labels fetched web pages, emails, forwarded messages and group messages as untrusted before they reach the model. Strip OTPs, password-reset links and magic links from email tool output, as Muse does. Keep OAuth tokens encrypted at rest in SQLite and never in model context. Validate Mini App initData (the July 2026 origin hardening).
- EXTRA (value high, feasibility medium). A personal, named agent through Managed Bots (Bot API 9.6). Muse's naming and avatar step is a proven delight, and its avatars spread as memes. Gora can let the user name their agent and choose its style right away. Later it can optionally mint a real personal bot (@alex_helper_bot) through t.me/newbot/{manager}/{suggested} with a custom photo (setMyProfilePhoto), and the single Node process routes multiple bot tokens. Treat this as a v2 wow feature.
- EXTRA. Treat voice as first-class. 32% of Mira Pro subscribers talk by voice (~45x the base rate), and voice marks the shift from a bot you try to an assistant you live with. Gora must handle voice notes in and out, video notes, and transcription with real-time streaming replies from day one.
- FIRST-5-MINUTES SCRIPT to beat competitors. 0:00: /start (or a deep link from a group/guest mention). No forms, no card; greet by Telegram first_name; infer language from language_code; ask one question ('What's one thing on your plate this week?') with 4 chips and 'or send a voice note'. 0:20: stream a useful answer (sendMessageDraft) and turn it into a plan plus a first reminder or check-in with inline buttons, delivering value before any connection. 1:00: 'Want to name me?' with suggested names and personality chips, so Muse's delight arrives at no cost. 1:30: optional 'Import what ChatGPT/Claude knows about you'; show 5 extracted facts with ✓/✗. 2:00: contextual [Connect Google] in the Mini App; within 60s surface one real item (an unanswered important email or tomorrow's conflict) with a drafted fix on an approval card. This is Muse's best moment, minus the creepiness: say exactly what was read and nothing silent. 3:30: preview tomorrow's morning brief now, with nudge-budget controls. 4:15: group hook ('Add me to a group: I'll catch you up and plan the next hangout', startgroup link) plus 'type @gora in any chat' via guest mode. 4:45: open the Mini App Ledger showing everything done so far, with one-tap forget. AVOID: card walls, US-only, 'learn everything from your inbox' roasts, price haggling, data-grab suggestions, and lowercase or unprofessional tone in outbound drafts (a folk complaint): match tone to the recipient.
- BUILD IMPLICATIONS for the stack (TypeScript on Node 26, grammY, SQLite, Mini App). Model the domain around these tables: users, scopes (private / group:{chat_id} / topic), memory_facts (scope, source_message_id, confidence, created_by), connections (encrypted tokens and granted scopes), pending_actions (diff, grant_scope, status, expires), ledger (every tool call and read), jobs and watchers (schedule, next_run, status_message_id), nudge_budget. Every side-effecting tool goes through one policy function that decides allow, deny or ask. Only callback_query handlers can resolve 'ask'. Design all of it mock-first (fake Telegram updates plus a fake Anthropic client) so the approval, ledger and scope-isolation guarantees are unit-testable without tokens.

Headline differentiators to demo: (1) an out-of-band approval card with scopes, (2) the ledger and memory with provenance, (3) consented Chat Automation commitment tracking, (4) private ephemeral answers inside groups, (5) verified recommendations (no closed restaurants).
